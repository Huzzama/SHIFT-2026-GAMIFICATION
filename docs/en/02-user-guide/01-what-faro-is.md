# 1. What FARO is

FARO is a companion for people who study online while they work. It does not teach the course: Canvas does that. What FARO does is answer three questions Canvas does not.

- **What do I do now?** One single next step, sized to the time you actually have.
- **How do I come back?** When you have been away, a new route instead of a list of overdue work.
- **Am I alone in this?** Other people working on the same thing, without having to talk to anyone.

The product promise is short: **hard to quit, easy to return.**

## What it is NOT

Worth saying, because it defines the rest of the guide:

- **It is not a video game.** It uses game mechanics, but there are no lives, no levels, no Game Over.
- **It is not a social network.** No followers, no ranking, no way to see who is ahead.
- **It does not replace Canvas.** Your grades, submissions and dates still live there. FARO only reads.
- **It is not a to-do list.** On no screen will you see "you have 14 overdue activities".

## Navigation

Six tabs at the bottom (or on the left when the window is wide). **FARO sits in the middle, raised**: it is the piece that connects everything else.

| Tab | Answers |
|---|---|
| **Home** | Where am I and what do I do now? |
| **Journey** | Where am I going? |
| **FARO** | The mentor: explains, organises, accompanies |
| **Community** | Who is with me? |
| **Impact** | Your semester's effort, in FARO points, and how close you are to your reward |
| **Progress** | What you have built |

Two more screens are not tabs and appear when needed: **Recovery**, which opens from Home when you come back from an absence, and **Profile**, which opens from Community. They are kept out of the bar on purpose — FARO must not feel like yet another app with sections to manage.
