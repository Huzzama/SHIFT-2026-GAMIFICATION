# 7. Community

Answers **who is with me?** It is a digital campus, not a social network. It is ordered by need, not by novelty.

## 1. Presence

The dark green strip with a pulsing dot: how many people are studying your course now, how many are enrolled, how many are in rooms.

They are **counts, never people**. And since nobody appears by name, neither do you: the screen says so explicitly.

## 2. Your profile

A button with your avatar. Opens Profile.

## 3. I'm stuck (SOS)

The amber button. It is the piece that best explains FARO: **three kinds of stuck, three different systems.**

| If you say… | FARO takes you to… | Because… |
|---|---|---|
| I don't understand the topic | **Mentor** | You need an explanation |
| I don't know how to organise my time | **Recovery** | You need a plan |
| I want to talk to someone | **Community** | You need people |

FARO does not answer every problem the same way, and Community is the answer to only one of the three.

## 4. Ask the community

Four kinds: **Question**, **I'm stuck**, **Something I learned**, **Tip**. Optional title, required text. It is posted with the context of your course and current module, so whoever answers knows what you are talking about.

## 5. Study rooms

Studying accompanied **without talking to anyone**. Each room shows a name, course, how many are inside, duration and mode: **quiet** or **pomodoro**.

When you join, the room takes the whole screen: a large clock, the avatars of those with you, and a line saying nobody sees whether you were productive — only that you were accompanied.

**Joining gives no points. Finishing does.** On completing the session: *"You studied alongside 5 people"* and +20 FARO points.

You can also **create a room** of 15, 25, 30, 45 or 60 minutes.

> In the prototype the clock runs fast (one second per minute) so a full session can be shown. The screen says so.

## 6. Community mission

A collective goal for the week — for example, 500 activities between everyone. Your bar adds to everyone's. **Nobody competes**: when it is completed, everyone who took part receives recognition, regardless of how much each contributed.

## 7. The feed

Filters: All, Asking for help, Questions, Learnings.

**How it is ordered** — and this is the most important thing on the screen: **reactions do not count**. The order is decided by relevance to your course and module, whether someone is stuck and unanswered, and how recent it is. A post with forty reactions and one with zero rank the same if they are equally relevant to you.

And someone asking for help weighs **more** than someone celebrating an achievement. Landing on a front page full of other people's wins is the worst welcome for someone having a hard time.

**Reactions** are about learning, not popularity:

|  | Means |
|---|---|
| ❤️ | I like it |
| 👏 | You did it |
| 💡 | It helped me |
| 🔥 | It motivates me |
| 🤝 | I'm with you |

They are shown **separately**, never summed into a single number. "12 applauses" says what happened; "12 likes" would be a scoreboard.

Each kind of post offers the reactions that serve it: someone asking for help is offered *I'm with you*, not applause.

**Replies.** Tap the counter to open the thread. Every reply carries its origin: **Peer answer**, **from the instructor** or **FARO explanation**. A peer answer with forty helpful marks **is still** a peer answer, and the thread says so. There is also **Report** on every post.
