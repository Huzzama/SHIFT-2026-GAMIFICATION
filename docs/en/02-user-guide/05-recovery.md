# 5. Recovery

The screen FARO exists for. It opens from the Home strip, or from SOS in Community.

**The rule that orders everything:** you are never shown everything you owe before being shown how you can finish.

## The order, and why it is that one

**1. Checking your progress…** — one second. Skipped if your system has reduced motion.

**2. Welcome back.** *"Your progress is still here. FARO recalculated your route."* Below, in small print: *"You were away 5 days."* It is a fact, not a reproach. It will never say "you lost 5 days".

**2b. Reconnect in 2 minutes** — if you have been away 3 or more days, a violet card appears: *"Three questions on what you saw last."* It is optional (*Not now* is always one tap away) and **it is not a test**: it does not count towards your grade and nobody else sees your answers.

When you start, the cards take the whole screen, one at a time, with three large options and a progress bar at the top (● ● ○):

- The questions are about **the modules you already worked on**, latest first. Never about something you have not seen.
- If you are right, the option turns green and the explanation appears.
- If you are wrong, the card shakes, the option is struck through, the key idea appears and **you try again**. There is no way to "lose".
- When the three are done, a closing screen: the flag *Review done*, the title **"You're back"** and *"You remembered more than you thought. Your journey is ready."* (if fewer than half were right first time: *"You went through all of it, and that is what counts today. Your journey is ready."*), **+75 FARO points** and the **Continue** button. Points are for finishing, not for first-try answers.

Only one set of cards is rewarded per day. Afterwards, the top of the screen keeps the mark *Reconnected today · +75 points*.

Why before the numbers? Because after days away, the first thing you need is not a plan but proof that you still remember something. Two minutes you can succeed at, before seeing how much is left.

**3. Your current situation** — three numbers, not eighteen lines: days until the close, remaining work in hours, modules left.

**4. Feasibility** — the honest answer to *can I still finish?* Four possible answers:

| Verdict | When it appears | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| **Yes, you can finish** | What is required fits comfortably in your time | Green, with the min/day needed |
| **You can finish by picking up the pace** | It needs a bit more than you sustain | Amber, with both numbers |
| **Your original route is no longer realistic** | It does not reach | Dark green, not red, and offers alternatives |
| **FARO needs more information** | No close date or no history | It does not guess |

The gauge compares what the course demands against what you sustain. The vertical line is 100%: crossing it means you are being asked for more than you said you have.

That third verdict is deliberate. FARO would rather tell you it does not reach and offer a shorter plan than cheer you on with something it cannot sustain.

**5. What happened?** — optional, and it changes what FARO asks of you:

| If you choose… | FARO… |
|---|---|
| I have less time | Shrinks your sessions to 10 minutes |
| I'm overwhelmed | Hides the pile and leaves you a single step |
| I don't understand the material | Offers to take you to the Mentor |
| I lost my routine | Prepares a comeback mission |
| I need a break | Activates the safe harbour |
| I'm ready to continue | Resumes the route |

None changes dates or academic rules. The screen says so at the bottom, in those words.

**6. How much time do you have?** — 15, 30, 45, 60 min/day or **it varies every day**. That last one is the important one for an adult with an unpredictable schedule: it produces a flexible plan with a minimum goal, a recommended one and optional extra time.

**7. Route options** — three paces over the same work: **Comfortable**, **Balanced**, **Intensive**. Each says how many days it needs and how much slack it leaves, computed, not promised. At most one carries **Suggested for you**, and only when there is an objective reason: it is the closest to what you already sustain. None is called "the best".

**8. Your new route** — the first three days, with a button to see the rest. If something does not fit before the close, an amber notice says so: FARO does not hide the work that does not reach.

**9. Why did FARO change your route?** — collapsible. The five inputs of the calculation. The AI is not a black box.

**10. Today's one thing** — one action. If it is longer than your sessions, the text acknowledges it: *"Start it and pause when you need to — moving forward counts."*

**11. After completing it** — *"Done. You are back on route."* with your new momentum, and two exits: continue on the Journey or reconnect with the Community. Coming back to the course and coming back to people are two different returns.

## Recalculating

Every time you change the time or the pace, **"Recalculating your route…"** appears and then **"Your route was updated"**. It is not decoration: the plan below is genuinely rebuilt.
