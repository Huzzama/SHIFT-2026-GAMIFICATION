# 2. First use: your destination

The first time you open FARO there are no tabs. Only three questions. Until you answer them there is nothing else — without a destination, FARO has nothing to plan with.

## Step 1 — Why are you studying?

Eight options: career growth, better income, career change, a promotion, finish my degree, personal achievement, personal development, something else.

You pick one. It is not a personality test or a psychological formula: it is personalisation. There will be a week when you don't feel like opening the course, and this is what FARO will remind you of.

## Step 2 — What would finishing it let you do?

A free text field. In your words, one line is enough. The example it shows: *"Lead my own projects instead of just executing them."*

That text later appears in Home, Journey and Progress as your destination. It is optional, but it is the part that makes the rest feel yours rather than a system's.

## Step 3 — How much time do you really have?

Five options: **10, 20, 30, 45 or 60 minutes** a day.

**This is the step that changes FARO's behaviour the most.** It is not an aspiration, it is a budget:

- The Next Best Step is chosen among the activities that **fit** in that time.
- Recovery's feasibility check compares the minutes the course demands against the ones you declared.
- The recovery route is packed into days of that size.

If you put 60 because it sounds good and you only have 20, FARO will plan a week you cannot keep. Be honest before ambitious: that is why the screen says it in those words.

## Afterwards

Your destination sits on a dark card with your goal and your sentence. Below it, the time chips stay editable: **you can change your available time whenever you want**, and everything else recalculates on its own.

To come back to this screen later: **Progress** → tap your destination card, at the bottom.
